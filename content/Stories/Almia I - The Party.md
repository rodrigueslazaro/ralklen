# Chapter 1 - The Party

The noon light illuminated the courtyard as the princess danced in flowing robes of fine green silk, jumping from arm to arm among her suitors as demanded the traditional dance. The music was jolly and loud, as was fit to a noble's birthday, and even more so for the princess herself, in her fifteenth birthday. All around the courtyard the noble houses of Tarin sat under the shadow of trees, although it was not a particularly hot day, yet in the middle of spring. There was no lack of foods to taste or wines to drink, *and yet people still find themselves with empty mouths to gossip lies*, thought Almia.

It has always been the way since Almia's first memories. In the entire country of Tarin anyone with any piece of land played politics, or at least tried to, and in the capital, Faris, it was tenfold worse. Scheming and backstabbing were the norm, both political and literal, and today it was of the worst kind as every house presented and exalted their suitors for the princess, trying to crawl their way into the royal bloodline.

She attended not with this goal, however, for her house, house Couto, had no unmarried youths. Her children were long married, and her grandchildren either too young or else betrothed. She had been invited because of the prestige of her house, which she worked hard her entire life to recover and even increase after her godfather's mistakes. And she would be damned if she let any fifthly lies taint her work.

Not even two years ago compliments about her 'not looking her age' or being 'beautifully untouched by time', began to disappear in conversations, being replaced by gossips of what she did to be so well preserved. Rumors as mysterious as 'taking imported concoctions from the west', as stupid as 'bathing in ice from the eastern mountains', or even as blasphemous as insinuating pacts with the Other, reached her ears. The problem was that not even Almia knew herself, and she was far from complaining that in her early sixties she still kept the look and energy of her late twenties.

Maybe it was just her imagination, but she did feel the eyes on her since she arrived, specially of the older guests. There was something there, a different tone when they talked to her. Once a rumor began it was hard to stop it, and even more so if it related to the great houses. Her house, house Couto, used to be a prestiged great house, and it still kept the title, but after suffering the leadership of Auro Couto, may Alad consume his soul, it had declined to barely greater than a common house of the farm lords.

Almia knew the power of rumors from the ones she herself fabricated, specially about house Martem, the worst abuser of Auro’s mistakes, who had bought Castle Icaro in the south of Couto’s State, claiming almost twenty percent of her house’s land. A few months back, however, Almia was delighted to find out that the common house that had moved into the castle, house Feia, was headed not by a Malkani, the people of Tarin and the neighboring states, descendants of the great Empire of Malkain, but a man from Valelhandir, the eastern country of farmers and grain merchants.

By the law of tradition, only pure blood Malkani could command Castles, and now she waited anxiously for the arrival of Mr. Busan, a Valelhandirani merchant who she hired to find proof of Lord Malco’s ascendants.

She was almost taken aback when her husband touched her arm, her thoughts returning to the party. With a warm smile he asked "Is everything alright, love?" And she replied, "Yes, of course," trying to smile back. Unlike her, Santes did look his age, and he was in his late sixties. He wore a brown and blue doublet with golden embroideries and a protruding shoulder pad, as did almost every other man in the party, although with different colors. "Look," he said, subtly pointing at the man coming their way with his head. He raised to receive the man, and she soon followed, *finally*, she thought.

"Mr. Busan, what a pleasure to receive you here," he said as he greeted the man and shook both hands. Mr. Busan was a tall and thin man with the common pointed chin and long nose of the Almani blood. He was dressed with common Valelhandirani party clothes, it's simplicity in colors, but not in shapes, contrasting with the Farisian style. Almia greeted him right after.

Almia awaited anxiously as Mr. Busan greeted the rest of the table, smiling from ear to ear and gesturing as only a Valelhandirani could: "What a pleasure it is to be here indeed. What a party, isn't it? The princess could not look more beautiful than today. I'm sure the lucky suitor who claims her hand won't tire of declaring it so." *He surely will not, having the influence of the Queen on his back*.

"Oh. Yes. Marcela is glowing today, whoever suitor she chooses shall be very glad," said Almia.

"Guilhere shall be the one, I'm sure. He's the most charming here today," said Almia's granddaughter, also named Almia after her. She sat opposite to Almia on the table, wearing a light blue dress with puffed shoulders and a thin adorned diadem on her forehead, as was common for women in the Farisian style. "Not to mention the best dancer." she finished, giggling with all the wisdom a twelve year old.

"Hah! Most charming? Only because I'm already betrothed," responded Jule with playful smugness. Jule was Almia's sister, sitting besides her two heads taller at his seventeen years of age. He whore an intricately embroidered coat with shoulder pads protruding in sharp angles. Jule was indeed a handsome young man, but the scar across his left eye granted him no compliments in the pristine expectations of nobility, that’s why his mother had to marry him off to a common house.

"As if..." Almia began, but her mother cut her off.

"Stop you both, don't fill our guest's ears with your nonsense," said Sinia, little Almia’s mother. She sat wearing a similar dress as her daughter’s, but yellowish in color and with more mature cuts. Despite Almia’s best efforsts, Sinia had never been her brightest child, but at least she could trust her, and she was certainly also anxious for Mr. Busan’s news.

"Oh don't you worry about me Lady Sinia, I enjoy the youngling's banter, you get used to it after your seventh child," he roared a short laugh. "I'm sure young Jule would be a fine suitor, but I heard more compliments about Malino than about Guilhermo," he said, mockingly whispering the last part for little Almia.

"Malino?! He dances like a duck with wooden legs!" She protested, Jule laughed.

"Why don't you come sit with us," said Sinia pointing at a free seat.

"I would surely love to," responded Mr. Busan, "but I believe I'd best gladden my good Lord and Lady Couto with a walk through this most beautiful garden. This is surely a most blessed spring, I've never seen so many colors in one place, hah!"

They walked out of the blue archways of the courtyard into the garden. It was paved with the same blueish stones that built the rest of the castle, and the knee-high flower arrangements were truly beautiful with pops of golden, purple, and all of the other colors of the rainbow. They were not the only ones looking for private conversation however, for all around nobles gathered in small groups talking in low voices, all pretending not to try to overhear one another.

When they were far enough away from the party, Mr. Busan, after taking a sip of his wine, changed the exchange of pleasantries for the real subject.

"It was no easy, but I'm glad to provide you with the proof you were looking for."

Almia was relived, it would work out. "Oh wonderful, how did you find it? In Cahora like we thought?" she Asked.

"Alas, it was not so simple. Valelhandir keeps extensive records of, well, everything, births and deaths included. But it was not in Cahora, the woman must have moved a lot around to avoid pursuit. Her children's logs were in Pinalia."

Cahora was the western most province of Valelhandir, bordering Tarenn on the south. It was the first clue she had gotten about Lord Malco’s past. "Interesting, she must've been truly desperate, that's on the other side of the country." said Almia.

"She must've. That's what made it take so long. But alas, the record is kept safe in my wagons."

"We thank you for your services, Mr. Busan," said Almia, "and we'll pay you most handsomely, don't you worry." Valelhandirani merchants sometimes profited more from the activities they carried out parallel to the trade of grains than the trade itself.

Mr. Busan smiled. "Yes, most surely. But as for the initial price... Well, it is good to have a bit of inter-harvest activities, but raising seven children with the luxuries they're used to has not been easy."

"It sure is not, but two hundred crowns is a generous offer, two times more than your harvest profit, and..." began Santes.

"It is a generous offer, yes, but I've had—other, offers. I long respected house Couto, and I do take you as friends, so I’m obliged to inform you, and I pledge you not to take this as any more than an opportunity that is impossible for me to ignore."

"What is it, Busan?" stated Santes.

Almia suddenly began to feel a little sick. *Rat bastard*.

“Lady Martem…” he began.

“That fifthly snake,” she burst out. Anger began boiling up inside of her.

“Lady Martem…” he repeated, “is one of the main sources of the gossips about your—blessings, Lady Almia, and of course she’s not shy of using it.”

The crone never hid her envy, her compliments always tasted of hexes. *What flawless skin, I hope it lasts long*. *It can be a problem when your experience is not reflected on your looks*. She already knew it had to be Lady Martem, all she needed now was to find what the woman had planned. “And what in the void did she want from you?” Asked Almia. 

“I’m afraid her works go beyond plain gossip. You know the legends never have a good thing to say about—well, for lack of better word, *immortality*. Vampires, witches, *inaris*, and the lot. With the right story, and evidence, she could have a case against you. The King does have a history of fear of the dark.”

‘*Immortality? That’s absurd!*’ She thought. She did not age as fast as others, of course, but she surely felt the weight of the years on her shoulders, even tho it was not much, and there was the sickness she got sometimes. ‘*But the fool king, what can he be led to think?*’ He seemed to believe in every old tale, many times leading men off in missions to hunt invisible monsters.

“Evidence, what evidence?” Asked Santes. “Almia’s blessing is from Dala.” 

“Indeed, I do not doubt it, not a second. But with the right stories, even the shadows may begin to sprout monsters.”

Almia grew impatient. “Again, what did she want, Busan? What did she offer you?”

“Only information, that’s all. And a… task, of sorts. To leave an item in your quarters in the keep. A golden coin, with an upside down triangle. Of course, I did not tell her anything, and would never do such a thing, but with an offer of three hundred for such a simple matter…”

“It’s not a simple matter, of course she knows of your relations with our house, this is valuable, much more valuable than wandering around in a wagon,” protested Almia.

“Oh yes, yes, there is value in trust. But I cannot ignore this offer, and…”

“You’ll receive three hundred and fifty, Mr. Busan. And I hope that you keep your word and your luck, for I don’t ever want to see you or your wagons near my house again, lest they become charcoal,” stated Santes.

Mr. Busan gave a short bow. “Always a pleasure doing business with you.” And left, casually walking around the garden to get back to the party through another entrance.

Almia turned to her husband. “What are you thinking?” She muttered angrily. “Mr. Busan has always been a valuable asset, we could’ve done more with him.”

“Valubale? Crowns are valuable, Almia, and we do not have that many that we can spare in a man who sells his honor to the highest bidder,” he responded.

Almia still felt a little sick, so she sat down on a wooden bench close by. The pungent scents of the flowers around her did not help, *and why is it so hot all of a sudden?* She could almost see the smells, undulating in her vision in colors of gold and purple. They seemed to merge together with the threads already weaving in her mind, the golden coin of Alad in her room, enough to accuse her meddling with the dark. The king pointing a giant finger on her face and sentencing her to death. The horror of her house being persecuted after she was gone, there was screaming and flowing blood.

“Are you all right, love?” Came a voice, faint and distant as if underwater. Bubbling up to reach her.

“Just that damn sickness again,” she managed. “I think I’ll…” turning around, she threw up on a bush of white roses. Santes held her shoulder and her hair, then he sat besides her as she turned around again, her mind slowly returning to her. She felt comfort as she pressed onto him, then counted to twenty before trying to do anything else, a technique she developed over the years. Each number cleared her mind more, and then finally she managed to open her eyes and rise. She cleaned her mouth with the rest of the wine in her hand and spitted it to the side, straightening herself again. *No time for weakness*.

“As always, it comes and goes,” she said.

“I’m glad it is so,” he responded while getting up. “If Lady Martem wanted to plant something in our rooms, she would not have asked only Busan, I think we should go back.”

“No, no, that would only raise more suspicions, we cannot leave the princess party this early. We’de better go back to the table,” she replied.

They did.

In the middle of the courtyard the dancing had stopped, and the band moved from a jolly to a more calming tune. The space was quite crowded as all of the great houses and even some of the smaller houses were invited. She found Sinia in a circle with Ladies Ilia Venta and Nina Ouvia, discussing this or that about crocheting. Inia was as young as Almia looked, she had a heart-shaped face and the kind of eyes that made men look twice. She wore a voluptuous green dress, an emerald diadem, and various golden bracelets. It was a pity she ended up with the husband she had. Nina, on the other hand, was even older than Almia, wearing a red dress, she held in her eyes that look of badly hidden envy.

“Ilia, Nina, good afternoon,” she greeted as she arrived. They replied gracefully.

“How’s your leg faring, Santes?” Asked Nina. “My husband told me of the accident in the Queen’s Forest.”

“Nothing but a scar now, thank Dala,” he replied.

“I always say dad should be more careful while hunting, specially as he’s not getting any younger,” said Sinia. Almia noticed the quickest glance from Nina.

“Oh, I’m sure it’s nothing to worry about,” began Ilia. “My dad hunted until he could climb up a horse, and he died in his sleep, probably because mother did not let him go hunting that week.”

“Jano also used to hunt,” said Nina, talking about her husband, “but he just does not have the energy for it anymore.”

“I won’t deny that it is as tiring as it is rewarding,” commented Santes.

“Oh, don’t try modesty with me. I imagine between the two of you there’s plenty of energy to spare. Almia, specially,” said Nina with a smile.

“It is astonishing, Lady Almia,” said Ilia, “I wish to be like you when I get your age.” Nina’s scornful glance at herself was not even hidden this time to Almia’s eyes.

“Ding ding ding—Ding ding ding,” came the sound of cutlery hitting a glass. “Attention!! Attention, everyone!!” Called a woman’s voice from the middle of the courtyard. The chattering stopped, and soon after the band with a last stroke of an untuned string.

Almia struggled to find a gap through the crowd, all turning curiously to see the woman. When she saw, her heart dropped. It was Lady Martem.

“What a wonderful party we have here today. It is a pleasure to see so many of our great and greatest houses here present.” She gestured in big and graceful movements as she spoke, wearing a constant smile of satisfaction in her wrinkled face. “The princess, specially,” she said turning to look at young Marcela watching her with the slightest tilt of her head to the right, “is looking fabulous. Oh; but what a Queen will she make! Our good King, blessed be his name in Kohan, must be so proud,” she looked at the fat King sitting behind her to the right and gave a short bow. He raised his chalice in response.

Almia froze while listening to the woman. She desperately wanted to run, but run where? To her rooms? She knew in her heart it had already been tampered with.

“But as it is with life, the party never lasts forever,” she saddened the tone of her voice, as well as her expression. “The beautiful gardens of our great Castle Bom, with this scented wind and beautiful colors, are not always this pretty. As much as we fight, pray, or hide, autumn always comes, leaving the gardens yellow and then dead. The winter always comes, and the night. How much do we pray for Dala, and Mala, and Kohan, and all of the Saints, that things may not be so? But as we all know, that’s just the way things are. The way things have always been. Except…”

Nina did not even try hide her scornful look at Almia now, the crone looked satisfied. *She knew about this*.

“Except,” she repeated, louder this time, “that there are exceptions to the rule. Things, and creatures, that in opposing the natural cycle of Dala, become constant, unchanging, *immortal*. The evil of the world is always constant, the depths of the void are always unchanging, and some creatures of the night are immortal, lest our good warriors and heroes smite them with Dala’s light!! It is our obligation, our duty, our wish, to always follow the nature of Dala, blessed be his name in our tongues. The nature of summer, and winter. Of planting, and harvesting. Of making and breaking. Of living, and dying.”

A hundred threads weaved inside of Almia’s mind, but she still kept her attention. She knew exactly what Lady Martem was getting at, and she would have to play her cards exactly right if she wanted to prove her innocence. *Nowhere to run now*.

“And I am afraid, my good Lords and Ladies. My good King, and Queen, and princess. That an evil, a creature of Alad himself, has infiltrated our most noble houses. Has infiltrated our lives, has infiltrated *this party*; and is standing right among us today!” The crowd gasped in horror. “This creature, despite looking as a young Lady, can only be a creature of the dark. An *inari*, to be precise. A creature draining the life force of younglings to keep its own youth. A creature that does not get sick, grow old, or decay. That creature, my Lords and Ladies, has a name, a name it uses to disguise itself as a Lady. And that name,” she pointed a long ringed finger in Almia’s direction, “is Lady Almia Couto.”
