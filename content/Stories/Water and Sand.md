
# Prelude - Water and Sand

— You'll never catch me! I'm the legendary Uosung!

He ran with all his might across the beach, leaving wet prints and splattering his chaser with sand. In the end, however, the Emperor was faster than the great Uosung, and he fell under the weight of his enemy, rolling over until both panted on the ground, looking at the sky with their arms stretched, sand mixed with sweat over their naked bodies.

As soon as they catched their breath they went into the water again, the game this time to look for new items to add to their collection, which was protected in the impenetrable sand fortress they had built earlier. Already they collected three colourful translucent stones, which they called emerald, diamond and ruby, and they were looking for a sapphire to complete the jewels of the Emperor's crown.

They did not find it, however, and in the end they settled for a starfish and two seashells, then they got back to their fortress. Not too long after expanding the walls, Binge got frustrated with the northeast tower that wouldn't stay up, to the point that the starfish became a dragon greedy for their fortified treasure, and so the gate wall tumbled down, and the rest of the construction soon after.

— I'm the Power, it's all mine! All mine! Said the dragon with the deepest voice Binge could make while he conducted it in a jumping dance over the debris.

Yize usually laughed at this play, Binge noticed, but he was not very well that day. Although his brother did not speak, Binge could always feel what was going on inside his head, sometimes this feeling even formed images in his own mind. Today, he was afraid, it had started from early in the morning, but it seemed to have only grown during the day, despite Binge's efforts.

They had not seen the time go by as they player all afternoon, so the sunset caught them by surprise. Already it glowed a deep orange, the water reflections made it seem like liquid metal.  Yize pointed towards the trees. Binge, who was playing with the ocean sound inside of the shells, hadn't heard his uncle at first. He got up, knocking sand out of his body then extending a hand to help his brother.

Binge grabbed the jewels and went straight to Jiong to show them off, but as he was about to talk, he felt the terror buiding up in his chest like a tsunami, making him turn around before his uncle said "What's with Yize? Go get him."

— Brother! Brother! He screamed as he ran to him, grabbing his shoulders which were suddenly very cold. Yize was paralysed with his eyes startled, he panted deep and fast with his jaw pressed tight. There was nothing Binge could do, so he hugged him close, it had helped before, he believed.

Suddenly Yize yelled high and loud, sounding even louder to Binge who had his ear so close to his mouth, but he didn't have the time to notice the sound, for the image that had formed in his mind was much more tormenting. It looked like a man with his eyes closed, but the next moment it jumped towards them with burning red eyes in a shroud of darkness.

As soon as it came it was gone, the curse did not last very long, as always. He felt warm tears flowing down his shoulder, and he couldn't help but let his eyes tear up as well, but he had to be strong for his brother.

— We're going home, he said after pushing him away and clening his own eyes with the back of his hand, mom is gonna know what to do, she'll help.

— Did it happen again? Asked their uncle.

Binge managed an answer with his head.

— Let us go then, the night comes. He took Yize first and put him in his front in the horse, then he felt the firm grip of their uncle on his ribs, lifting him up to the back. After his uncle got up in the horse again he hugged on firmly as they went home.

It were a few hours later into the night when Binge found himself in the basement of the village's old temple. The sound of the rain was faint in the distance while a cold draft came down the stairs, making the few candles twist and dance, almost dying before lighting up again.

His mother was sitting in a stool besides the jumble of blankets that covered his brother, letting only his sleeping face show through. When they got there he scribbled down his visions in a frenzy on the scraps of scroll the Minister had provided, and he got very tired after that.

The Minister was a chubby wrinkled old man with a bald head and a long braided beard. He wore the dark green vest of the Empire's Heralds, and the medallion of the Order down his chest. He had helped his family since the first time the curse started, when they were five.

His uncle was a tall man with a shaved head, except for at the top, where he left a long pony tail that traileed down his back. It was the hair of the warriors, but that was not the only thing that showed it. He had broad shoulders and thick arms, and he wore a battered old brigandine.

They were leaning over the table trying to make sense out of Yize's scribbles. In one scrap there was a series of pairs of dots in a straight line. Binge recognized it as once as an army of the red-eyed men, so they were trying to piece together the rest.

— A mountain, what would you say? A volcano, actually, said the Minister putting the shape that looked like fire on top of the triangle.

— Are you sure these are the men you were talking about? Asked Jiong with his deep voice.

— It is, confirmed Binge.

— A ship was seen earlier out of its normal route to the east, his uncle continued, this is not a mountain, he turned the volcano upside down, it's a ship.

— They are coming in the ship, Binge jumped to drag the eyes to the deck.

— But it cannot be, said the Minister in a hushed tone.

— What is it? What do you make of this circle?

— This, he said taking the scrap on his hands, is the symbol of the faith used in the elder days, when our people lived yet across the sea.

— The symbol of balance, but the dark side is coming over the edge of the curve, pondered his Uncle.

— But they are only legends, aren't day? No, no, my boy is never wrong, there must be a mistake, said his mother worriedly as she came to the table.

— It is said in the holy scripts, though no one really enjoys remembering this part, that the hand, he said dragging the figure that looked like flames to the top of the ship, of the corrupted ancestors would once more rise to test our strenght.

The lost ones, Binge remembered the name from the horror stories they were told so they wouldn't leave the house at night, or go into the forest alone. Stories, that was all, but he had seen it, and his brother had felt it worse still. He was looking straight into the sea when the curse happened.

— They are coming tonight, announced Binge to the room, his stomach was cold and weird, and it seemed to be growing through his body, the ship you said, uncle, it was close, Yize was looking at it, just over the horizon, probably.

There room went quiet for a moment. His mother ran her fingers through her hair and paced around, while his uncle scratched his beard pondenringly. The Minister was the first to break the silence.

— I'm afraid the boy may be right.

— Can we fight them? Asked Binge. In the stories of the scripts they fought the lost ones back in the beginning, before their people fled across the sea.

— We can't risk it, there may be hundreds, thousands of them coming, we must flee, everyone.

— Flee? They won't believe a word we say, brother, it'll be like the last time.

— The Circle of Steel will come and bring their families with them, we'll have to convince the rest.

The wind and the rain made so that the only hope for light were the weak oil lanterns spread across the room. They were huddled inside of the long main hall of the temple. Yize was still asleep besides their mother.

Jiong had left not thirty minutes earlier, and now all the men who were in the Circle of Steel were waiting with their wives and children. Some were asleep, while others had scared faces. They were only told that they had to flee. If they knew what was coming like Binge did, would they be as controlled as he was?

He had to be strong, and he was showing that know, if anyone noticed. He would be in the Circle one day, train with the sword and lance to protect his family against danger, but he did not know if he would be able to fight the lost ones now that not even his uncle believed he could.

His mother went away to grab something, then came back with an extra blanket, which she trew over him.

— Thanks, but I'm not cold.

— It's windy, keep it on.

And so he did, and waited patiently in the temple like everyone else. It were maybe ten or another thirty minutes later when anything happened.

— That's propostorous, you're just fomenting anarchy in our people! Came the voice of the Mayor angrily, it grew from the first word until in the last one he entered through the temple's double door. He was wet from head to toe, water dripped from his clothes, his uncle who came right behind was in the same situation.

— What is the meaning of this? This is no time to be out and about, do you truly believe this lunatic? What does he have as proof besides the words of a child!?

— My boy saved your family from the fire! His mother rose and rebuked, he's always right, we need to leave.

— The guards saved my house from the fire, the words, not even that, the scribbles of your crippled child had nothing to do with it!

— Jiong, Jiong! Came the voice of a man rushing towards the temple. The ship has docked!

In that moment every man and woman jumped from the benches, grabbing their children and any supply they had brought.

— Everyone, grab your horses and your carts, we're leaving now! Boomed his uncle.

They rushed to the back of the temple where the horses were leashed. Those families who had two were quickly on their way, while those who did not even had one shared the few carts. Yize had woken up in his mother's arms, and he tried to explain everything that was going on while they went away on a cart under the rain.

The main road to the village went south towards Ienzen, the biggest town of the region, and as it was the only path that could take wheels, so that's where they went. Binge sat besides Yize under the blanket that was now heavy with water, the moon was just a brighter spot on the starless sky, leaving the road pitch black.

He tried to get this mind away from imagening what was happening in his village. His uncle hadn't reached them yet, and he was starting to get very cold. At some point and odd sound started to grow towards them.

— Is it hailing? He asked his mother.

— It's not the time of year for that.

Suddenly a new sound appeared, it were the hoofbeats of horses coming towards them. Binge saw the bulk of the man riding the first horse and knew it was his uncle, behind him came a handful or other men, probably from closed counsel, the highest rank of the circle.

He was relieved for a moment, but then two things happened at once. The sound of hail turned out to be in fact rolling gravel, and a cloud of sand and dust reached them together with his uncle, hitting his eyes and making them blink furiously, forcing him to turn his head in the opposite way.

— Forsake the carts! Climb your horses and go! Run!

The cart they were in stopped, the man that was conducting it got down to undo the knot that tied the horse to the cart.

— Come on you two, climb in here, said Jiong halting besides the cart. The dust storm was still coming so it war hard to see, but Binge made sure his brother climbed the horse first before he jumped behind.

Another man waited on the other side, close to his mother, it was Beien, the fishing master.

— Go with him.

— No, I can't leave them Jiong!

— There's no time Hua, we must go!

Reluctantly, she climbed the other horse, the owner of the cart had already left with his family, and so did most of the people, leaving ten or twelve abandoned carts ahead on the road.

— Where did this storm came from? His mother asked when they were already on the move.

— The storm came with them, but we're ahead, just keep moving, explained Jiong.

The sandstorm hit their Binge's back more strongly the more they tried to run, he was holding tight to his uncle's back, trying to stay calm under his fear. He felt like he was being watched, but there was no way to look without getting sand in his eyes. He decided to glimpse really fast, and he knew that was a mistake.

He only managed a brief moment of vision before his eyes got hurt again, but that was enough to get him to urge his uncle. Like in his brother's drawing, there was a line of pairs of red eyes reflecting the few light there was in a red glow.

— They're coming! He yelled, then felt his uncle pressing harder on the horse's sides.

But he felt the sound coming louder behind them anyway, running steps over the wet gravel, they would all perish.

Suddenly his uncle halted and jumped from the animal, leaving Yize to take to bridle.

— Circle, assemble! He shouted, and all the other man jumped from their horses to join him.

He slapped their horse and it went away with all speed, their mother joined besides them, and all the other horses with women and children fled with them. The last sound Binge heard was angry growls and screaming men.
