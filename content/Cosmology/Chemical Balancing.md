
Chemical balancing is the process by which an *unbalanced* weave, that is a weave with different amounts of Soul and Chaos, becomes *balanced*, that is a weave with equal Soul and Chaos. To understand this process one must first be familiar with [[Chemical Notation]].

The entire goal of the cosmos is to balance Sive and Dive, and thus every interaction of threads seeks balance, and nothing that is unbalanced stays so for very long, this is specially noticeable in material weaves.

Taren has a unique property of always carrying with it a thread of either Intent or Impetus, as well as of being interweaved with all of the other threads of Charge, that is Xenir, Nazem, and IJi, as represented below:

![[Material Weave Split.png#invert|350]]

This represents the material weave without any Intent or Impetus, which makes a perfectly balanced weave because the sum of all Soul is the same as the sum of all Chaos. Taren + Xenir + Iji carry 6 Soul and Nazem + Xenir + Iji carry 6 Chaos.

As stated above, however, Taren is always accompanied by some other thread of Intent or Impetus, but when it does, it generates an unbalanced weave. For example, the unbalanced weave of Pure Water below, where Taren has interweaved with Wala, Impetus Ring. As always, the loop Wala brings around it a field of Deik, its analogous thread.

![[Unbalanced Water 1.png#invert|420]]

This weave is now unbalanced because the sum of the fundamental threads is not equal:
- Taren + Xenir + Wala + Iji + Deik = 11 Soul
- Nazem + Xenir + Iji = 6 Chaos

At this point there are multiple ways to balance this weave, depending on where this unstable weave formed in the universe and what other threads were around it. The forms of balancing are Natural Balancing, Metallic Balancing, and Pair Balancing, as detailed below.

## Natural Balancing

If the threads or weaves needed for the other forms of balancing do not exist around the unbalanced weave in the moment of its formation, one of the following processes will take place to balance the weave, depending on the initial state.

### Taren+Ring Balancing

If Taren interweaves with a Ring, like Wala, the disparate thread Nazem will increase to be able to receive the field of the analogous thread, in this case Deik from Wala, a process called *disparate accommodation*. 

Because Nazem increases, and a loop always brings with it a field of its analogous thread, in this case Iji, it will flow into Wala, because it currently has only one outgoing flow, and is thus vulnerable to an incoming flow, finishing the balancing process by *parallel balancing*.

This same process happens in the formation of Natural Mercury, starting with Intent Ring Deren.

![[Natural Water Formation.png#invert|500]]

In chemical notation, this process can be described as follows:

1) In the Unbalanced Water we have:
	- Taren 3s
	- Wala 3s
	- Deik 2s
	- Xenir 2s1c
	- Iji 1s2c
	- Nazem 3c
	- Total Soul = 11
	- Total Chaos = 6
	- Status: Unbalanced

2) After disparate accommodation takes place we have:
	- Taren 3s
	- Wala 3s
	- Deik 2s1c
	- Xenir 2s1c
	- Iji 1s2c
	- 2 Nazem 3c
	- Total Soul = 11
	- Total Chaos = 10
	- Status: Semi-balanced

3) After parallel balancing takes place we have:
	- Taren 3s
	- Wala 3s
	- Deik 2s1c
	- Xenir 2s1c
	- 2 Iji 1s2c
	- 2 Nazem 3c
	- Total Soul = 12
	- Total Chaos = 12
	- Status: Balanced

If Taren interweaves with a Flux, like Asor, both Taren and Nazem will have to increase at the same time to be able to accommodate the potential field of Asor, the process of *parallel accommodation*.

Because both Taren and Nazem increase and the weave currently has two fluxes and one rush, Nazem evokes a second rush of Iji to balance the weave, the process of *parallel balancing*.

## Summary of Terms

- *Parallel balancing*: the process by which a flux emerges to balance a parallel flux.
- *Disparate accommodation*: the process by which the disparate of the flux thread will increase to accommodate the incoming flow.
- *Parallel accommodation*: the process by which both opposite loops increase at the same time to accommodate a flux.
