
Falmora is the only rocky object with surface water and an atmosphere. It has a diameter of 2 million kilometers and rotates on itself every 4800 hours. As it rotates, it faces different moons and constellations. Because of the uneven distribution of star energy in the star zone, some quadrants are warmer than others, causing the different seasons on the surface of Falmora.

The seasons are marked by their moons and constellations, those being:
- Summer: Constellation of Harienir, Ikia Moon;
- Fall: Constellation of Dawn, Adala Moon;
- Winter: Constellation of the Prophets, Hera Moon;
- Spring: Constellation of the Dragon, Sum Moon.

The planet of Falmora is covered in the upper atmosphere by a layer of cyalite, a light magnetic super-gas which lets light pass when going through it one way but blocks some of it when coming from the opposite way. Inside Falmora there is a rotating magnetic field with a speed of 20 hours per cycle perpendicular to the rotation of the planet, this magnetic field aligns with the cyalite, causing it to block some of the light from the stars, causing night time. This night time effect is only present on median latitudes, fading at the poles where the magnetic lines run more parallel to the cyalite layer.
